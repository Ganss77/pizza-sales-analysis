A stable business with clear room to grow

Sales were steady all year, with no month dropping off a cliff. The upside is in three places: the dead hours after lunch, the slow Sunday and Monday, and group orders, which already bring in almost 40% of revenue. The menu also has a long tail that adds complexity without adding money.

Sources

4 tables, one star schema

The core of the model is order_details: one row per pizza line in an order. It links to orders (when), pizzas (size and price) and pizza types (name and category).

Every relationship is one-to-many, and the joins in my queries follow them exactly: USING(order_id), USING(pizza_id), USING(pizza_type_id).

Key rule: pizzas sold = SUM(quantity), not the row count. Counting rows would miss 954 pizzas.

SELECT
    od.order_id, o.date AS order_date, o.time AS order_time,
    strftime('%m', o.date) AS month,
    CASE strftime('%w', o.date)
        WHEN '1' THEN 'Monday'   WHEN '2' THEN 'Tuesday'  WHEN '3' THEN 'Wednesday'
        WHEN '4' THEN 'Thursday' WHEN '5' THEN 'Friday'   WHEN '6' THEN 'Saturday'
        ELSE 'Sunday'
    END AS weekday,
    strftime('%H', o.time) AS hour,
    pt.name AS pizza_name, pt.category, p.size,
    CAST(p.price AS REAL)        AS unit_price,
    CAST(od.quantity AS INTEGER) AS quantity,
    ROUND(CAST(od.quantity AS INTEGER) * CAST(p.price AS REAL), 2) AS revenue
FROM order_details od
JOIN orders o       USING(order_id)
JOIN pizzas p       USING(pizza_id)
JOIN pizza_types pt USING(pizza_type_id);

Finding 02 / 08
Monthly trend

Sales are stable all year round

13%
gap between the best and worst month

July was the strongest month ($72,558 from 1,935 orders), and October the weakest ($64,028). Month-to-month changes stay within ±10% and there is no strong seasonality. September–October is the quietest stretch, so that's when a campaign would help most.

Monthly revenue, $K below: change vs previous month
WITH monthly_sales AS (
    SELECT
        strftime('%Y-%m', o.date)  AS month,
        SUM(od.quantity * p.price) AS revenue,
        COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id) AS total_orders
    FROM orders o
    JOIN order_details od USING(order_id)
    JOIN pizzas p         USING(pizza_id)
    GROUP BY month
)
SELECT
    month, revenue, total_orders,
    ROUND((revenue - LAG(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY month)) * 100
          / LAG(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY month), 2) AS growth_pct
FROM monthly_sales
ORDER BY month;

-- strongest and weakest month
SELECT month, revenue
FROM monthly_sales
WHERE revenue = (SELECT MIN(revenue) FROM monthly_sales)
   OR revenue = (SELECT MAX(revenue) FROM monthly_sales)
ORDER BY revenue;

Workload

Friday peaks, Sunday is quiet

70.8

Friday is the busiest day of the week. Sunday is the quietest (50.5 orders, 29% fewer), and the other days sit between 57 and 62. Each day has two peaks: lunch 12–1 pm (~7 orders an hour) and evening 5–7 pm. In between, 2–4 pm is a clear slump.

SELECT
    CASE strftime('%w', date)
        WHEN '1' THEN 'Monday'    WHEN '2' THEN 'Tuesday'
        WHEN '3' THEN 'Wednesday' WHEN '4' THEN 'Thursday'
        WHEN '5' THEN 'Friday'    WHEN '6' THEN 'Saturday'
        ELSE 'Sunday'
    END AS day_of_week,
    ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) * 1.0 / COUNT(DISTINCT date), 2) AS avg_orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY day_of_week
ORDER BY strftime('%w', date);

SELECT
    strftime('%H', time) AS hour,
    ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) * 1.0 / COUNT(DISTINCT date), 2) AS avg_orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY hour
ORDER BY hour;

WITH pizza_sales AS (
    SELECT
        pt.name,
        SUM(od.quantity * p.price) AS revenue,
        SUM(od.quantity)           AS total_sold
    FROM pizza_types pt
    JOIN pizzas p         USING(pizza_type_id)
    JOIN order_details od USING(pizza_id)
    GROUP BY pt.name
)
SELECT 'Top 5 BY REVENUE' AS metric_type, name, revenue, total_sold
FROM (SELECT * FROM pizza_sales ORDER BY revenue DESC LIMIT 5)
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Top 5 BY QUANTITY', name, revenue, total_sold
FROM (SELECT * FROM pizza_sales ORDER BY total_sold DESC LIMIT 5)
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Bottom 5 BY REVENUE', name, revenue, total_sold
FROM (SELECT * FROM pizza_sales ORDER BY revenue ASC LIMIT 5)
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Bottom 5 BY QUANTITY', name, revenue, total_sold
FROM (SELECT * FROM pizza_sales ORDER BY total_sold ASC LIMIT 5);

L brings in 45.9%, M 30.5% and S 21.8%, while XL and XXL together make up only 1.8%. The categories are well balanced, from 23.7% (Veggie) to 26.9% (Classic), so revenue doesn't depend on any single category.

SELECT
    pt.category,
    SUM(od.quantity * p.price) AS revenue,
    ROUND(SUM(od.quantity * p.price) * 100.0
          / SUM(SUM(od.quantity * p.price)) OVER (), 2) AS pct,
    SUM(od.quantity) AS total_sold
FROM pizza_types pt

Menu tail

Brie Carré is the one to cut first

1.42%
of revenue · only 490 sold all year

Brie Carré sells half as much as any other pizza. Next in line are Green Garden, Spinach Supreme, Mediterranean and Spinach Pesto: together the bottom 5 make up 8.8% of revenue ($71.8K). Items 2–10 perform almost the same (~930–1,000 units, 1.7–2.1% each), and the bottom 10 together = 18.8%.

10 weakest pizzas: revenue share ■ critical · ■ weak · Σ = cumulative share
SELECT
    pt.name,
    ROUND(SUM(od.quantity * p.price) * 100.0
          / SUM(SUM(od.quantity * p.price)) OVER (), 2) AS pct,
    SUM(od.quantity) AS total_sold,
    ROUND(SUM(SUM(od.quantity * p.price))
              OVER (ORDER BY SUM(od.quantity * p.price)) * 100.0
          / SUM(SUM(od.quantity * p.price)) OVER (), 2) AS running_total_pct
FROM pizza_types pt
JOIN pizzas p         USING(pizza_type_id)
JOIN order_details od USING(pizza_id)
GROUP BY pt.name
ORDER BY pct;

WITH order_summary AS (
    SELECT
        od.order_id,
        SUM(od.quantity * p.price) AS order_revenue,
        SUM(od.quantity)           AS total_pizzas,
        CASE WHEN SUM(od.quantity) >= 4 THEN 'Large orders'
             ELSE 'Small orders' END AS order_type
    FROM order_details od
    JOIN pizzas p USING(pizza_id)
    GROUP BY od.order_id
)
SELECT
    order_type,
    COUNT(order_id) AS total_orders,
    ROUND(COUNT(order_id) * 100.0 / SUM(COUNT(order_id)) OVER (), 2) AS orders_pct,
    SUM(order_revenue) AS revenue,
    ROUND(SUM(order_revenue) * 100 / SUM(SUM(order_revenue)) OVER (), 2) AS revenue_pct
FROM order_summary
GROUP BY order_type;

Promotion windows

Two quiet windows are ready for a promo

+$38.9K
yearly upside of two promos at +10%

After lunch, revenue almost halves: from $111.9K at 12:00 to $53.0K at 15:00. The 2:00–4:59 pm window brings in $182,249, so +10% = +$18,225. Sunday and Monday are the weakest days ($206,533 combined), so +10% = +$20,653.

SELECT
    strftime('%H', o.time)                      AS order_hour,
    ROUND(SUM(od.quantity * p.price), 2)        AS current_revenue,
    COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)                  AS total_orders,
    ROUND(SUM(od.quantity * p.price) * 0.10, 2) AS potential_extra_revenue
FROM orders o
JOIN order_details od USING(order_id)
JOIN pizzas p         USING(pizza_id)
GROUP BY order_hour
ORDER BY order_hour;

07 · Limitations
An honest look at the data

What the data doesn't tell us, and how to fix it

Limitations

  • Only one year (2015): no year-over-year comparison, so seasonality isn't confirmed.
  • 358 of 365 days have orders. 7 days are missing, likely holidays or closures.
  • No cost data: this report covers revenue, not profit margin.
  • No customer or channel data: repeat customers, delivery vs dine-in are invisible.
  • +10% is a scenario, not a forecast. The promo impact still needs testing.

Next steps

  • A/B test the promos for 4–6 weeks against control weeks.
  • Add ingredient costs to measure margin per pizza.
  • Basket analysis: which pizzas are bought together, to design combos.
  • Hourly demand forecast to plan staff shifts.
  • Collect 2016 data and repeat the trend analysis.
